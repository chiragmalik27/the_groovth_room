# The Grovvth Room --- Website Structure, Content & Design Rules

**Project document:** Frontend website planning brief\
**Source of truth:** *The Grovvth Room --- Service Deck Descriptions*\
**Purpose:** Guide the website's information architecture,
section-by-section content, visual direction, interaction rules, and
quality standards.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Project objective

Create a polished, credible corporate website for **The Grovvth Room**
that clearly communicates its business growth and enablement services.

The website should help visitors understand:

1.  What The Grovvth Room does.
2.  Which types of businesses or founders it may support.
3.  What the eight listed services cover.
4.  How a visitor can enquire or start a conversation.

The website must feel intentionally designed and brand-led---not like a
generic AI-generated template.

### Positioning direction

Based on the service deck, The Grovvth Room can be presented as a
**business growth and enablement consultancy** spanning business
foundation, strategy, brand and market development, distribution,
marketing, pilot validation, technology enablement, and founder
assistance.

Suggested positioning line for exploration:

> **Your partner in building, launching and growing businesses.**

This is proposed website positioning, not a verified company slogan.
Obtain client approval before publishing it.

    "referrals" or "introductions", retain that distinction. Do not

-   Company history, founding date or founder biographies.

# 4. Homepage: section-by-section flow

The homepage should tell a coherent story. Each section should answer a
visitor question and lead naturally to the next section.

## Section 01 --- Header / Navigation

**Purpose:** Help visitors orient themselves and reach important pages.

**Content:** - Official logo. - Primary navigation links. - One clear
CTA such as "Let's Talk".

**Design and behaviour:** - Clean, compact header. - Consistent spacing
and alignment. - Clear hover, focus and active states. - Sticky
navigation only if it improves usability. - Mobile menu must be easy to
open, close and navigate.

**Rules:** - Use the supplied logo; do not recreate or distort it. - Do
not place every page link in the header. - Do not use multiple competing
CTA buttons. - Do not make the header excessively tall. - Ensure the
menu works with keyboard and touch input.

## Section 02 --- Hero

**Purpose:** Explain the company's broad value proposition immediately.

**Suggested content direction:**

Headline option:

> Building businesses with clarity, strategy and purpose.

Supporting copy direction:

> The Grovvth Room supports founders and businesses across business
> strategy, brand development, market readiness, marketing, distribution
> and technology enablement.

Suggested CTAs: - Explore Our Services - Let's Talk

**Visual direction:** - One strong, relevant image or a considered
typographic composition. - Strong headline hierarchy. - Plenty of
breathing room. - A clear focal point. - Avoid clutter behind the text.

**Rules:** - The first screen must make the company's purpose
understandable. - Do not use vague claims such as "Unlock limitless
potential". - Do not invent a market-leading position or business
results. - Do not use random AI-looking abstract blobs, floating
dashboards or unrelated stock images. - Do not place long paragraphs in
the hero. - Get the final headline and supporting copy approved by the
client.

## Section 03 --- Brand introduction

**Purpose:** Explain what The Grovvth Room is and the kind of business
challenges it addresses.

**Content direction:** - A concise introduction to the business growth
and enablement model. - Explain that the service offering spans business
foundation, strategy, market development, marketing, distribution and
technology. - State the intended audience only after the client confirms
it.

**Suggested heading direction:**

> More than a single service. A connected approach to business growth.

**Rules:** - Keep the copy specific and readable. - Do not repeat the
hero paragraph. - Do not claim a track record, client base or years of
experience without evidence.

## Section 04 --- Three broad capability pillars

**Purpose:** Make the eight services easier to understand before showing
the full list.

The following grouping is a proposed website navigation device, not an
official classification from the source deck.

### Pillar A --- Build

Possible related services: - Legal Assistance & Business Registration -
Business Strategy & Growth Planning - One-on-One Founder Assistance

Short description direction: Establish the foundations, structure and
direction of a business.

### Pillar B --- Grow

Possible related services: - Marketing & Digital Growth - FMCG Brand &

### Pillar C --- Enable

Possible related service: - Technology & Digital Enablement

Short description direction: Use digital tools, systems and integrations

**Rules:** - Keep the three pillars visually distinct but part of one
design system. - Link each pillar to the relevant services. - Do not
suggest these are the company's official departments unless confirmed. -
Do not force an artificial equal number of services into each pillar.

## Section 06 --- How we work / Our approach

**Purpose:** Give visitors a clear understanding of how an engagement
could progress.

**Proposed process for client validation:**

1.  **Understand** --- Discuss the business, objectives and current
    challenges.
2.  **Assess** --- Identify relevant needs, opportunities and
    constraints.
3.  **Plan** --- Define a suitable strategy, priorities and workstreams.
4.  **Coordinate** --- Align the required activities, people and
    partners.
5.  **Review** --- Evaluate progress, feedback and agreed measures.
6.  **Refine** --- Identify next steps and opportunities for
    improvement.

**Rules:** - This is a proposed process inferred from the service
offering. The client must confirm that it reflects actual operations. -
Do not promise fixed timelines or outcomes. - Explain coordination and
third-party involvement accurately. - Keep the process easy to scan;
avoid a complicated flowchart.

first-time founders. - Early-stage businesses. - Established businesses
exploring growth. - FMCG brands preparing for market entry or
expansion. - Businesses seeking digital and operational enablement.

**Rules:** - Confirm the company's preferred customer segments with the
client. - Present these as audience types, not as verified client

or geography unless confirmed.

## Section 08 --- Why The Grovvth Room

**Purpose:** Explain the company's actual differentiators.

**Content to request from the client:** - What makes its approach

**Rules:** - Do not invent differentiators. - Do not use unsupported
phrases such as "India's No. 1", "industry-leading" or "trusted by

## Section 09 --- Proof / Case studies / Testimonials (conditional)

**Purpose:** Build trust through evidence.

**Use only if the client supplies verified material:** - Client-approved
project summaries. - Genuine testimonials and attribution. - Approved
logos. - Measurable outcomes with context and permission. - Real

**Rules:** - Never fabricate client names, logos, quotes, numbers or
project results. - Obtain permission before publishing identifiable

**Rules:** - Publish only useful, reviewed content. - Do not create fake

# 6. Visual design direction

## Recommended direction: Modern editorial corporate

The design should feel thoughtful, composed and premium without becoming
overly decorative.

### Suggested visual characteristics

-   Strong typographic hierarchy.
-   Restrained colour palette.
-   Generous but purposeful whitespace.
-   Editorial and asymmetrical layouts where appropriate.
-   Clear grid and alignment.
-   High-quality, relevant photography.
-   Subtle motion and interaction.
-   Consistent spacing and component behaviour.

### Exploratory palette

Use this only as a starting point until the client confirms brand
colours:

  Role            Suggested colour    Hex
  --------------- ------------------- -----------
  Primary         Deep forest green   `#172D27`
  Background      Warm ivory          `#EAE7DC`
  Secondary       Sage                `#A8B69C`
  Accent          Muted gold          `#C6A77B`
  Text            Deep charcoal       `#202522`
  Light surface   Soft off-white      `#F7F6F1`

Check contrast before using any colour combination for text or controls.

### Typography direction

Possible pairings: - Display/headings: DM Serif Display or Playfair
Display. - Body/UI: DM Sans or Manrope.

Use no more typefaces than necessary. Confirm font licensing and load
fonts efficiently.

### Photography direction

Prioritise: 1. Genuine company and team photographs. 2. Approved
photographs from real work. 3. Carefully selected stock photography with
a consistent art direction. 4. Custom illustrations only where they
explain something useful.

Images should relate to founders, business planning, product
development, retail, distribution, marketing or technology. Avoid
unrelated decorative images.

# 7. What NOT to do

This section is mandatory. Use it as a design and development review
checklist.

## Avoid generic AI-template patterns

-   Do not use the same card layout for every section.
-   Do not put every section inside a rounded rectangle.
-   Do not use purple/blue gradients by default.
-   Do not add random glowing orbs, blobs, floating shapes or decorative
    dashboards.
-   Do not use glassmorphism everywhere.
-   Do not use excessive shadows, borders or pill-shaped elements.
-   Do not use generic icons as a substitute for meaningful visual
    design.
-   Do not make every section centre-aligned.
-   Do not make every heading oversized.
-   Do not repeat the same three-column grid throughout the page.

## Avoid generic or misleading copy

-   Do not use empty phrases such as "Unlock your potential",
    "Revolutionize your business" or "Take your business to the next
    level" without specific supporting meaning.
-   Do not invent statistics, client logos, testimonials or case
    studies.
-   Do not claim guaranteed sales, leads, rankings, funding,
    product-market fit or business growth.
-   Do not claim years of experience, awards, certifications or market
    leadership without proof.
-   Do not describe partner-delivered work as in-house execution unless
    confirmed.
-   Do not overstate legal, tax, compliance or other regulated
    capabilities.

## Avoid poor user experience

-   Do not use autoplay video with sound.
-   Do not use intrusive pop-ups on first visit.
-   Do not make essential information dependent on hover.
-   Do not hide important service information behind complex
    interactions.
-   Do not use tiny text or low-contrast colour combinations.
-   Do not create a navigation menu that is difficult to use on mobile.
-   Do not add animations that delay reading or navigation.
-   Do not let animations cause layout shifts or motion discomfort.
-   Do not use buttons that look clickable but do nothing.

## Avoid poor technical implementation

-   Do not build the entire website as one giant component.
-   Do not duplicate service content in multiple places; keep content
    structured and reusable.
-   Do not use unoptimised, oversized images.
-   Do not ignore mobile layouts.
-   Do not leave broken links, empty routes or placeholder text in
    production.
-   Do not expose API keys or secrets in frontend code.
-   Do not collect enquiry data without an actual submission path and an
    appropriate privacy notice.
-   Do not add analytics or tracking without the client's approval and
    suitable privacy handling.

# 9. Accessibility, SEO and performance rules

## Accessibility

-   Use semantic HTML landmarks and heading order.
-   Provide meaningful alt text for informative images.
-   Ensure keyboard navigation works.
-   Provide visible focus indicators.
-   Label every form field.
-   Use sufficient colour contrast.
-   Respect reduced-motion preferences.
-   Make tap targets comfortable on mobile.
-   Do not communicate status through colour alone.

## SEO

-   Give each page a unique title and meta description.
-   Use one clear primary heading per page.
-   Use descriptive URLs and service slugs.
-   Add appropriate Open Graph metadata.
-   Use descriptive internal links.
-   Add structured data only when the business details are verified and
    the markup is accurate.
-   Do not stuff keywords into copy.
-   Do not publish duplicate service descriptions across every page.

## Performance

-   Optimise and resize images.
-   Prefer modern image formats where supported.
-   Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery.
-   Avoid unnecessary JavaScript and animation libraries.
-   Prevent layout shifts by defining image dimensions/aspect ratios.
-   Test real mobile performance.
-   Check Core Web Vitals before launch.
